# EnaGIS — Final PRD and Project Description

**Team Dynamis · OSEAS 2026 · Assessment and Mapping of Productive Uses of Energy (Agriculture)**
**Status:** Final consolidated version, 4 Oct 2026. Supersedes PRD V1. **One decision is open:** the commodity (§6).
**Key dates:** commodity decision 6 Oct · ranking freeze 16 Oct · MVP freeze 21 Oct · defense prep 22–25 Oct · event 26–27 Oct (Kigali)

---

## 1. One-line and 30-second summary

**EnaGIS tells energy teams which farm collection points to investigate first, and how sure we are.**

After harvest, products need energy for cooling, washing, drying or milling at specific collection points, and nobody has a complete list of those points. EnaGIS uses open geodata to estimate which collection points likely need the most stationary energy, compares that with documented capacity, and produces a decisive, ranked shortlist. Each rank carries an evidence trail and a confidence level, and a frozen sample of top picks is verified on the ground. It is **stage 1 of a funnel: screen (EnaGIS) → phone-check → site visit and business case.**

## 2. Problem, user and core question

- **User:** a productive-use-energy planning or site-assessment team with a fixed field budget (assume about 10 site visits) that cannot visit everywhere.
- **Core question:** *Given limited resources, which agricultural aggregation points should be investigated first for productive-use energy opportunities, and how confident can we be?*
- **Three questions EnaGIS answers**, each with its own evidence and validation:
  1. Where is agricultural product being aggregated and processed? (nodes, catchments, throughput)
  2. Where do facilities exist or likely exist? (registry and siting model)
  3. Where do estimated energy requirement and documented capacity appear mismatched? (gap buckets)
- **What it is not:** not a continuous energy map, not a detector of everything, not a dashboard of many layers, not a business case, not proof of bankable demand, not an LLM product.

## 3. OSEAS alignment

| Challenge deliverable | EnaGIS response |
|---|---|
| Open-source ML/GeoAI model or pipeline that detects, classifies or predicts agricultural processing or storage facilities | **Committed facility-siting experiment** (§8.3): presence-only, sector-level, spatial hold-out by district group, compared with baselines, reported win or lose. Imagery detection of small assets is rejected on resolution and label grounds and documented as a tested negative. |
| Interactive map or dashboard highlighting clusters of productive energy use or critical energy gaps | Static interactive map: ranked nodes, gap buckets, node card with evidence, uncertainty, energy context and a pre-visit checklist. |
| Documentation: datasets, approach, validation, accuracy, scaling | Method report, data manifest, validation report with intervals, limitations, and commodity-agnostic scaling notes. |

**Mentor guidance we rely on:** free choice of country (start small, generalizable); no ground truth is provided; one value-chain step in depth beats breadth, and downstream (post-harvest) is especially welcome; demand inference is valid when small assets can't be seen; judges weigh rigor and reproducibility over a flashy dashboard; closed or non-reproducible tools are the biggest pitfall; honest limitations beat inflated accuracy.

## 4. Principles

1. **Evidence labels on every output:** OBSERVED (reported by a named source, with source, date and licence; not verified truth), PREDICTED (model output), ESTIMATED (calculated from assumptions), INFERRED (combined evidence), UNKNOWN (never defaulted).
2. **Missing data is not absence.** No claim of "no facility here" from non-detection. Documentation density is a context variable.
3. **Ranges, not points.** Every demand figure is a distribution.
4. **Unlabeled is not negative.** Presence-only modelling; negatives only from officer-confirmed complete lists or verification.
5. **Every complexity must earn its place** by changing the ranking or the decision, shown against baselines.
6. **Open and reproducible:** pinned inputs, one-command rebuild, no closed tools, no network at runtime.
7. **Honest scope:** a screening shortlist, not a feasibility study.

## 5. Architecture

```
Raw open data (pinned, hashed)
 -> registry (manual + sources, precision classes)
 -> production layers (finest admin level)
 -> mode-aware road graph -> isochrone scenarios (dry/wet)
      |-> label-free covariates -> SITING EXPERIMENT (parallel branch)
      |-> capacity-constrained allocation -> energy requirement -> gap buckets
             |-> baselines B0/B1/B2/pop + hindcast
             |-> Monte Carlo (tiers) + value-at-stake screen
 -> freeze (git tag + shortlist hash) -> verify -> report
```

**Unit of analysis:** the candidate aggregation node (collection centre, washing station, cooperative, market or warehouse), not a pixel. Nodes carry a type and an existence status.

## 6. Commodity decision (OPEN, decide 6 Oct)

**Milk is NO-GO for an open-data pilot.** The feasibility audit failed checks A, B, D and F:
- RICA publishes monthly registers and an online public register. My recount gives roughly 30–35 distinct centres across about 10 districts. Fields are operator, district, registration number and dates. There are no sector locations, coordinates or capacities.
- Per-centre throughput was not found. National figures conflict (213 million L/yr in one NISR survey against about 1.2 billion L/yr elsewhere), and reported cooling capacity (483,000 L/day) and utilisation (about half) are aggregate only.
- Formal flow is small, and existing capacity is underused, which weakens a "capacity shortfall" thesis for milk.

**Fallback audits (parallel, one per team member; candidates are hypotheses, not findings):**
1. Coffee washing stations (tea factories secondary)
2. Horticulture or perishables cold chain (markets and packhouses as nodes)
3. Rice or maize milling and hulling

**Selection rule (apply mechanically):**
- At least 60 geolocated facilities of a processing or storage class at sector level or better.
- Capacity or throughput for at least 30 facilities (hindcast fallback: district totals).
- A documented verification pathway and one named contact.
- Processing happens at the aggregation point (so throughput approximates stationary need).
- Evidence that energy cost is plausible relative to product value.
- Take the first candidate that passes. If none passes, run the pipeline with the best candidate and report the siting experiment as a documented feasibility result, with demand inference and verification carrying the claim.

**Impact of switching away from cold chain:** the cooling-demand module is replaced by a service-class processing-energy model (for example kWh per tonne for washing, pulping and drying). This is about one to two days of rework. All other modules stand.

## 7. Requirements

### 7.1 Core (must exist at MVP freeze)
1. **Open registry** with per-record source, date, licence, geocoding precision class (P1 coordinates ~100 m; P2 named place; P3 sector; P4 district; P5 unlocated), capacity as stated with unit, status as stated.
2. **Facility-siting experiment** (§8.3), unconditional.
3. **Throughput hindcast** against reported facility volumes, with a stated fallback.
4. **Capacity-constrained catchment allocation** with one travel-time method and two to three assignment variants as scenarios.
5. **Physics-based energy requirement** with ranges (§8.4).
6. **Three supply states** and gap buckets (§8.5). No facility count is ever converted into capacity.
7. **Monte Carlo tiers** (Robust / Contested / Verify-first), about seven parameters.
8. **Baselines** B0, B1, B2, population and an expert shortlist, scored against outcomes.
9. **Wet/dry scenarios** and peak/off-peak throughput, with a best visit window on the node card.
10. **Frozen verification** (n ≥ 12) with decomposed failure modes and intervals.
11. **Decisive top-10** plus pre-visit checklist.
12. **Static map, node card, shortlist file** (CSV/GeoPackage).
13. **Clean-machine rebuild** with data manifest, CI smoke test and limitations report.

### 7.2 Important (if time allows)
Documentation index (two indicators), energy-context second axis, value-at-stake screen, registry audit in a reference district, structural node-set scenarios, isochrone bands.

### 7.3 Stretch
Building-cluster candidate nodes, supplier-register catchment validation, second-district transfer test, OSM contribution and open release of the verified sample, Sobol analysis, Bayesian calibration of the formal-channel share.

### 7.4 Cut (will not build)
Imagery CV model, continuous demand heat layer, composite scores, LLM chatbot, numeric value-of-information, server database, framework web app, Earth Engine in the critical path, multi-commodity build.

## 8. Method

### 8.1 Nodes and registry
Documented facilities plus rule-based candidates (trade centres, markets, OSM places). Every node has a type and an existence status. A gap at a node that is not a real aggregation point counts as a false gap. Only node types that typically perform stationary processing enter the demand model. Transit-only nodes are flagged "logistics-only" with demand UNKNOWN.

### 8.2 Catchments and allocation
- **Network:** a dated, hashed OSM extract; the graph is mode-aware (foot, bicycle, motorbike, vehicle) and includes tracks and paths as slow edges. Speeds are scenario parameters (OSM speed tags are sparse).
- **Seasons:** dry and wet scenarios with surface-based speed multipliers (unpaved and track slower, some impassable). Isochrones are precomputed at 3–5 discrete speed scenarios.
- **Allocation:** finest available admin-level statistics, labeled as modeled (district production OBSERVED; spatial allocation ESTIMATED; catchment production ESTIMATED). Nearest-node by travel time is the default; the assignment is **capacity-constrained** so overflow shows as unserved production and overlapping catchments are not double-counted.

### 8.3 Facility-siting experiment
- **Unit:** sector (about 416 sectors); point-level only if at least 60 facilities are geocoded within about 1 km.
- **Target:** documented in an authoritative list. Presence-only: unlabeled sites are never treated as negatives.
- **Features (label-free):** accessibility-weighted production, population, road class and junction degree, town status, distance to the nearest processor, night lights. Never allocation, capacity, demand or gap outputs (these leak labels).
- **Models:** regularised logistic regression; gradient boosting only if justified. Spatial CV by district group.
- **Metrics:** recall at the top 10–20% of units, Boyce index, block-bootstrap intervals; baselines population-only, production-only and nearest-neighbour.
- **Pre-registered keep/kill:** lower confidence bound of the margin over the best baseline must exceed zero. Thresholds are written before results are seen.
- **Role:** an independent test of the catchment logic (does accessibility-weighted production improve siting beyond population and road access?), a registry-completion tool (flags "check for an undocumented facility"), and a probability that a node hosts stationary processing. It never multiplies demand for documented nodes.
- **Limits:** it learns where programmes built facilities, not need, and it measures recall only against documented facilities. If positives are too few (fewer than about 60 across at least 6 blocks), report that as a feasibility result.

### 8.4 Energy requirement
Energy requirement is technical, not bankable demand.
- **Affine form:** E = V · e_unit + E_standing (standing and auxiliary loads rise sharply per unit at low utilisation).
- **Peak power** from batch structure (batch volume, specific heat, temperature change, efficiency, allowed pull-down time), not daily energy ÷ 24.
- **Dwell time and processing-at-node fraction** by node type (transfer point about zero; processing facility high), each a range in the Monte Carlo.
- **Service class output:** kW class, phase requirement, operating hours, peak and trough ratio (load-factor proxy).
- **Sanity floor:** thermodynamic minimum for any cooling case; field coefficients must sit above it.
- **Sourced:** production statistics with method, facility capacities, vendor ratings. **Calibrated:** formal-channel share (hindcast), energy intensity (survey or meter data, requested). **Assumption distributions:** efficiency, ambient temperature, standing loss, batches per day, season factor.
- **We do not claim:** absolute kWh/day better than a factor of about 2–3 per node, node-level peak kW, bankable demand, whether a documented facility works, power reliability, or which facility farmers choose.

### 8.5 Supply states and gap buckets
- **Supply states:** (1) no documented asset; (2) documented asset, capacity known (units resolved: tank volume vs daily throughput); (3) documented asset, capacity unknown.
- **Buckets:** **Undocumented supply** (not "known insufficient"), **Undersized** (capacity known and below capacity-constrained requirement; read against utilisation norms), **Verify first** (capacity unknown). Use "unserved requirement", not "energy gap". A signed margin is kept, not only max(0, ·).
- **Documentation index:** modulates the claim label ("low documented supply" vs "evidence of low supply"), never the numbers. It is validated against the verification sample.

### 8.6 Economics screen
**Value-at-stake:** energy cost (kWh/day × published tariff) as a share of the value of product handled (volume × price). A low share suggests affordability is plausible; a high share is a red flag. Requires open price and tariff data (check availability). Willingness to pay is not estimated; "current energy spend" is on the verification checklist.

### 8.7 Energy context (second axis, not in the ranking)
Grid proximity class, any available planned-extension or mini-grid layers, and solar resource, all labeled low-confidence (grid proximity does not prove access). Shown as "intervention class to investigate" (grid, mini-grid, solar hybrid) and a "verify with utility" flag for nodes near planned extensions. The planner can filter on these.

### 8.8 Uncertainty
- Joint Monte Carlo (quasi-random sampling) over about seven parameters: formal-channel or marketed share, production statistic and allocation proxy, assignment rule and spoilage or access threshold, node existence, turnover and capacity definition, seasonal factor, energy intensity.
- **Tiers, not percentages:** Robust (top-10 in most draws), Contested, Verify-first. Rank sensitivity is analysed on rank and top-k membership; magnitude sensitivity is reported separately.
- "78% in the top-10" means stable in 78% of plausible parameter draws under our stated ranges; it is not a calibrated real-world probability.
- **Output rule:** a decisive top-10 ranked by top-10 inclusion probability, with each uncertain node carrying the single question that resolves it.
- **Removed:** detector-recall parameter, Sobol on all parameters, multiple k values, numeric value-of-information, rank intervals beyond the top ~30.

### 8.9 Baselines
B0 (district production rank), B1 (production within a Euclidean buffer), B2 (road-catchment production), population, and an expert shortlist (ask an agricultural officer for ten places). Each arm is scored against the same outcomes: hindcast skill and confirmed hits in top-k on the verified sample. Retain a component only if it moves the top 10 by at least two nodes and does not worsen hindcast or verification. Different is not better: only validation shows better. If a simple baseline matches EnaGIS, report it.

## 9. Validation

| Level | Method | Realistic n | Supports | Does not support |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Node existence | Phone-check candidates | 20–30 | Precision of candidates | Recall |
| Allocation/catchments | Hindcast vs reported volumes; supplier-origin registers | ≥15 volumes; 5–10 registers | Rank skill vs baselines | Absolute volumes |
| Throughput/demand | Reported volumes; meter data | 3–5 metered | Plausibility of intensity range | Ranking truth |
| Registry | Audit entries; officer-confirmed lists in reference districts | 20–30 entries | Registry precision, local recall | National completeness |
| Final prioritisation | Frozen-ranking verification | 12–20 | Precision@k with intervals | Generalisation |

- **Freeze then verify:** git tag `ranking-v1`, shortlist hash, seeded sample (stratified across top, middle, bottom, plus the disagreement set and siting-flagged candidates).
- **Claim defined before looking;** failure modes decomposed: node does not exist, undocumented supply exists, demand negligible or too seasonal, confirmed.
- **Power:** 12 of 15 confirmed gives roughly 55–93% (Wilson); 6 false of 20 gives roughly 15–52%. Report n and intervals.
- **Ethics:** consent, standard form, validation data kept private, aggregates published.

## 10. Data and tooling

**Data leads (to confirm, not facts):** NISR surveys and sector boundaries (HDX), regulator and export-board registers, programme reports, HOT/OSM roads and HeiGIT road-surface data, open building footprints, land-cover and population rasters, regulator tariff schedules. Every dataset is recorded in `data_manifest.csv` (source, date, URL, SHA-256, licence, redistributable, processing step). Licence-restricted layers stay out of the public rebuild. OSM-derived outputs follow ODbL.

**Stack:** Python 3.12 (pandas, geopandas, shapely, pyproj, rasterio, scipy, scikit-learn, matplotlib), a pinned OSM extract with a file-based graph builder, GeoParquet, DuckDB for ingestion only, `uv` lockfile, pydantic-validated YAML config with units in names, Makefile, pytest, GitHub Actions CI, ruff, optional Dockerfile. Map: MapLibre with a vendored, offline OSM-derived basemap; plain GeoJSON for nodes and catchments. No server, no framework, no Earth Engine.

**Reproducibility requirements:** pinned environment and seeds; hashed data snapshots; manual steps as versioned CSVs (who, when, precision); licence manifest; spatial block definitions and split seeds; pre-registration via git tag; smoke-test CI on a tiny fixture; clean-machine rebuild by someone who did not write the pipeline; code and data licences chosen.

## 11. AI-assisted development protocol

All code is written by AI. Humans write specs, tests and decisions, review, and do data and outreach work.
- **Day 1:** interface contracts (schemas for registry, nodes, catchments, allocation, demand, shortlist), a `CLAUDE.md`/`AGENTS.md` rules file, toy fixtures with hand-computed answers.
- **Task cycle:** owner writes a task card → AI writes tests first, then code (one task per session, ≤ ~300 lines) → `make test` and `make lint` → a different member reviews → optional adversarial review by a fresh AI session → merge and log judgment calls in `docs/decisions/`.
- **Reviewer checklist:** lon/lat order and CRS before distance or area; units asserted at boundaries; row counts after joins; no silent fillna or dropna; UNKNOWN never defaulted; mass conservation; seeds recorded; CV grouped by district; no hardcoded constants; tests not weakened.
- **Human-only decisions:** parameter ranges and sources, gap definitions, thresholds, verification protocol, interpretation.
- **Guardrails:** stop an AI fix loop after 45 minutes and rethink the spec; twice-weekly explain-back sessions by each understudy; disclose AI use in the README.

## 14. Defense plan

**Pitch structure:** problem → the question → method (one slide) → three evidence panels (hindcast, registry audit, verification table with n and intervals) → live shortlist and node card → honesty slide ("what we don't know") → next steps. Figures come from `make figures`.

**Hero example:** the first node we would visit, what a production-only ranking would have picked instead, and what we found when we checked.

**Four judge questions:** *So what?* (show baseline vs EnaGIS and the verified outcome.) *Where is the AI?* (siting experiment with spatial CV and a prospective check; imagery rejected for stated reasons.) *How do you know?* (three evidence panels.) *Why not QGIS?* (capacity-constrained allocation, tiers, registry, measured validation; and if B2 equals the full model, we say so.)

**Expert objections and answers:**

| Objection | Answer and change |
|---|---|
| Demand is not willingness to pay | We rank technical requirement and screen affordability with a value-at-stake ratio; willingness to pay is a verification item (current energy spend). |
| Uncertainty pushes the decision back to the planner | A firm top-10; uncertain nodes carry the single question that resolves them; measured against the expert shortlist. |
| Seasonality and roads | Dry/wet scenarios, peak/off-peak throughput, best visit window. |
| Throughput is not need (transfer hubs) | Node typology, processing-at-node fraction and dwell time; transit nodes score zero. |
| Supply-side blind spot | Energy-context second axis, planned-extension flag, "verify with utility"; we do not claim viability. |

**What EnaGIS does not claim:** willingness to pay, viability, bankable demand, absence of facilities, or that a documented facility works. **Test applied to every answer:** does it change the output, and can we show that on real data? Run one ablation per objection and report how much the top-10 moves.

## 15. Definition of done (MVP)

Open registry with provenance and precision classes · siting experiment with spatial CV, reported win or lose · hindcast against baselines · capacity-constrained requirement with ranges and three supply states · frozen, verified shortlist with decomposed failure modes and intervals · decisive top-10 with pre-visit checklist · node card and shortlist file · clean-machine rebuild passing in CI · limitations report.

## 16. Known limitations (to state openly)

Open registries are thin and programme-driven; no ground truth exists, so validation rests on small samples; speeds, processing fractions and formal-channel shares are assumptions with stated ranges; the siting model learns documented siting, not need; energy context cannot show access or reliability; national statistics can conflict by large factors; verification answers are self-reported.
